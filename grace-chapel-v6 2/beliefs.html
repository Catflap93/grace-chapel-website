Confession of Faith

We hold to The 1689 Baptist Confession of Faith (available here), and its modern version, The Baptist Affirmation of Faith 1966.

Why hold to a confession of faith?

“The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience”, so proclaims the first paragraph of the 1689 Confession of Faith. With that in mind, why should a confession take an important role in a local church? If the Scriptures are to be our only infallible rule, what authority can a confession have?

The Confession itself answers that question. It says that all controversies of religion and all opinions of men are to be examined by the Holy Scripture (Chapter 1.10). That includes the Confession itself. We hold to it because we believe it faithfully sets out what the Scriptures teach.

Does the Confession help us understand the Scriptures? Yes. As the Confession itself says, some passages are clearer than others, and the things necessary for salvation are taught clearly enough for ordinary people to understand them (Chapter 1.7). When we find a passage difficult, we look to other passages that speak more clearly (Chapter 1.9). The Confession helps us do this by bringing together, in a coherent way, the doctrines we find throughout Scripture.

1. A summary of what the Scriptures teach

The first benefit of the Confession is having a summary of what the Scriptures teach about a variety of doctrines in one place, so that we can easily turn to it. The first chapter speaks about the Scriptures, the second about the triune God, the third about His decree, and so on.

The Confession provides Scripture proofs, which send us back to the Bible to see for ourselves whether what we read is biblical. For example, a Christian struggling with assurance can turn to Chapter 18 and see from Scripture how a true believer may wait a long time for assurance, or have it shaken. It is a blessing to have an accessible summary that helps us know where to look in the Bible, whether we are studying a doctrine for the first time or need encouragement ourselves.

2. A guardrail for our own thinking

Having a confession gives us something to compare our own theology with. We might sit down with our Bible and ask, “What does this passage say to me?” But we are fallen creatures who often make mistakes. How do we know whether we have understood the passage rightly?

The Confession gives us a time-tested summary, shaped and agreed upon by many experienced men of God. If we come to a conclusion that differs from the Confession, we should seriously ask why and go back to the Scriptures. And if we as a church are persuaded from Scripture that we have confessed something wrongly, we ought to correct it. In this way, the Confession helps guard us from falling into unbiblical ideas.

3. Unity in the local church

A shared confession helps us know what we believe together and gives us reason to rejoice in those truths together. Christ Himself calls His people out of the world and into local churches, where they build one another up and gather for public worship (Chapter 26.5). The Confession helps us see what we have in common as we do that.

This unity in the local church also leaves room for brotherly love and fellowship with Christians who differ from us. The Baptists who drew up the Confession agreed that baptism is for professing believers. As the original appendix explains, they differed among themselves about how that conviction should affect church communion. They left that question open in the Confession and urged one another to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

4. Clarity about what we undertake as members

The Confession speaks of members who “willingly consent to walk together, according to the appointment of Christ” (Chapter 26.6). At Grace Chapel, our Confession sets out the faith we profess as a church, and our Family Covenant expresses how we undertake to walk together. The Confession also helps the congregation know what it can expect from those entrusted with teaching.

Walking together sometimes means warning or correcting one another. The Confession reminds us that the church exercises discipline according to the rule of Christ (Chapter 26.12). A shared confession helps us understand what we have undertaken together. Scripture governs when discipline is called for and how it should be carried out, with repentance and restoration in view.

5. A clear account of our faith for others

A confession makes clear to others what the church believes. That was one of the purposes of the First London Baptist Confession of Faith in 1644. Those Baptist congregations wanted to answer charges of error and show their agreement with other Christians, while making clear where they differed. Their differences mattered, and clarifying them was important.

The Second London Baptist Confession, first published in 1677 and publicly endorsed in 1689, served a similar purpose. The Baptists used much of the wording of earlier Reformed confessions to make their agreement plain, and they invited Christians who differed from them on baptism to read what they believed. Our Confession still serves that purpose today. Anyone wanting to know what we believe at Grace Chapel can read it, examine it in the light of Scripture, and speak to us about it.

6. Keeping our course over time

A confession also helps a church maintain its identity and keep its course as pastors, officers and members change. It gives us a clear account of the faith we have received and want to pass on to those who come after us.

These are some of the reasons we hold to a confession of faith at Grace Chapel. We want to keep going back to the Scriptures, to understand them better, and to walk together in the faith they teach. As we do so, we have much to encourage us in our struggles and much for which to praise God.

Our Family Covenant

In coming into membership we covenant with each other, having entered into a family. We therefore agree to the following:

As regards to our conduct towards each other, we esteem it our duty and privilege to walk with each other in all humility and brotherly love, to watch each other's conversation, to stir up one another to love and good works, not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, but as we have opportunity, to worship God according to His revealed will, and when the case requires, to warn rebuke and admonish one another according to the rules of the gospel. Moreover, we think ourselves obliged to sympathise with each other in all conditions into which God in His providence may bring us. We also ought to bear with each other's weaknesses, failings and infirmities, and particularly to pray for and with one another that the gospel and the ordinances may be blessed to the edification and comfort of our souls whilst we admire and adore the grace which has given us a place and a name in God's house better than that of sons and daughters.

Summary of Our Confession of Faith

  1. We believe in the one true and living God, in three Persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, who is invisible, personal, omnipresent, eternal, dependent on none, unchanging, truthful, trustworthy, almighty, sovereign, omniscient, righteous, holy, good, loving, merciful, long-suffering and gracious.
  2. We believe that Almighty God has revealed all that is necessary to life and salvation in the sixty six books of Holy Scripture which are the Word of God. All Scripture was given by inspiration of God, is infallible and inerrant, and is the final arbiter in all disputes. Its authority is derived from its Author and not from the opinions of men.
  3. We believe that God made our first father Adam perfect, holy and upright. He was appointed representative and head of the human race thereby exposing all his offspring to the effects of his obedience or disobedience to God’s commands.
  4. We believe that Adam fell from his original righteousness into sin and brought upon himself and all his offspring death, condemnation and sinnership.
  5. We believe it is utterly beyond the power of fallen man to love God, to keep his laws, to understand the Gospel, to repent of sin or trust in Christ.
  6. We believe that God, before the foundation of the world, for his own glory did elect an innumerable host of men and women to eternal life as an act of free and sovereign grace. This election was in no way dependent upon his foresight of human faith, decision, works or merit.
  7. We believe that God sent his Son into the world, conceived of the virgin Mary by the Holy Spirit, unchangeably sinless, both God and man, born under the Law, to live a perfect life of righteousness, on behalf of his elect people.
  8. We believe that God’s Son died at Calvary to effect propitiation, reconciliation, redemption and atonement for his elect people. God bore testimony to his acceptance of his Son’s work by raising him from the dead.
  9. We believe that God’s Son ascended to the right hand of his Father and is enthroned in glory, where he intercedes on behalf of his people and rules over all things for their sake.
  10. We believe that God the Son has poured out the Holy Spirit to work alongside the preached Word. The Spirit of God regenerates the elect sinner and draws him irresistibly to faith in Christ the Saviour.
  11. We believe the elect, who are called by grace, are justified in the sight of God on account of the imputed righteousness of Jesus Christ which is received by faith alone.
  12. We believe that such as are regenerated, called and justified shall persevere in holiness and never finally fall away.
  13. We believe that baptism by immersion and the Lord’s Supper are Gospel ordinances belonging only to regenerated believers.
  14. We believe that the local church is under the authority of Christ alone. The communion of saints, however, requires recognition of and fellowship with other churches.
  15. We believe that the Lord Jesus Christ shall come again to raise the dead, both righteous and unrighteous, and that the righteous shall enjoy everlasting life and the wicked endure everlasting punishment.
