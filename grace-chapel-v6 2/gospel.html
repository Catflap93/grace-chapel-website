What Is the Gospel?

What does the word “Gospel” mean, and why does it matter?

What Is the Gospel?

Perhaps you've heard the word "Gospel" mentioned before. We talk about "the Gospel truth". But what does that word mean? And why do we keep on about it?

Well, the word Gospel means Good News. It is something good, and it is something to share. And the thing we are sharing is the message from God, about a problem that all mankind has, and yet that God has fixed in the person of Jesus Christ.

So the Gospel is all about Jesus Christ.

Perhaps you knew that already. Perhaps you said when you looked at the question at the top of this page, "Well, the Gospel is that Jesus Christ was born at Christmas and died at Easter" (or something like that), and you wouldn't be far wrong. The life of Christ, and particularly His death on the cross and resurrection from the dead, is central to the whole message (1 Corinthians 15:3–4).

But that's not the start nor the end of the matter.

Who Is God?

We should actually start with God Himself. The Creator of all things. We start by saying, "There is a God".

Right, but many people say there is a god, or there are gods.

True, but no god is like the God of the Bible.

First, He's not just like a superhuman. He's not just like a really powerful, really wise, really good man.

Unlike mankind, He is holy, meaning He is utterly unlike anything He has created, far above us in majesty and glory (Isaiah 6:3).

Unlike mankind, He has no faults, no flaws, no sins. He is perfectly pure and holy, and cannot approve of anything sinful or evil (Habakkuk 1:13).

Yet, He is also love (1 John 4:8).

So, is that why God created things, because He wanted something to love and someone to love Him?

No, this is another thing about God: He is Triune. There is One God, but He exists in three Persons. The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19).

From eternity, each Person has existed in perfect love and fellowship with the others. God has never been lonely, nor has He ever needed anything outside Himself (John 17:24; Acts 17:24–25).

God wanted to make His glory known, displaying His goodness, wisdom, power, and love, and so He created mankind in His image (Isaiah 43:7; Genesis 1:26–27). That means we were made to know God, to reflect something of His character, and to live in fellowship with Him.

The Problem of Sin

This is where the problem comes in.

God created everything very good, and originally mankind did enjoy a relationship with God. But then Adam and Eve rebelled, they disobeyed God, and they lost that relationship (Genesis 3).

Now all mankind descended from them has been born separated from God, with a sinful nature. We do not merely live in a broken world; we ourselves are sinners, naturally turning away from God and disobeying His good commands (Romans 5:12; Ephesians 2:1–3).

We call this the Fall, and it has affected the whole of God's creation.

People don't get along, and even start wars. It is difficult to produce food, and sometimes there are famines. People get sick. And the biggest one of all... everyone dies.

Death was not part of God's originally good creation but, now it is a just punishment for sin (Romans 6:23).

It might sound harsh, or that God has gone too far, but think about how indignant you feel when you hear of someone doing something really evil. Well, remember how we said God is far above us, and is pure? That is why God does not look kindly upon sin.

God has set a good law in place, something that would even benefit men if they obeyed it, yet men do not obey it. Therefore God will judge everyone for their sins, just as human judges judge other men (Romans 2:6; Hebrews 9:27).

Everyone breaks God's law (Romans 3:23). Now, that does not mean we are all as bad as the most evil person you can think of. But even one sin makes us guilty before God (James 2:10).

You cannot say to a judge, "Yes, I know I did the thing I am being accused of, but look, it's the only law I've ever broken". Even if that were true, you would be admitting guilt, and therefore have to pay the price.

Neither can you say, "Yes, I did steal that £100 from that person. But then I felt bad about it and gave it to charity". You still broke a law, and so still have to pay the price.

Or, somebody has to pay it for you.

The Good News of Jesus Christ

And this is the Good News.

Jesus Christ came into the world to pay the price for every sin of every person who would ever believe in Him (1 Peter 3:18).

You see, we could never pay back God for our sins. Our sins are not simply wrong things we have done to other people. They are offences against the infinitely holy God who made us. We cannot undo our guilt, and no amount of good works can satisfy His perfect justice.

But Christ was the Son of God manifested in the flesh! (1 Timothy 3:16).

That means He is truly God and truly man.

Because He is truly human, He could stand in our place as a suitable substitute, obeying God's law perfectly and suffering in our place. And because He is truly God, His sacrifice has infinite worth, completely sufficient to save all who trust in Him (Hebrews 2:14–17; 7:25–27).

He alone was the perfect man, never committing a sin Himself (Hebrews 4:15).

He lived the perfect life that we have failed to live, and died the death that our sins deserved.

Which meant that when He died on the cross, He was suffering a death He had not earned, something He did not deserve.

He died, though He was completely without sin. And by His death, He paid the price for the sins of His people. He satisfied God's justice, bearing the punishment that they deserved (Isaiah 53:5–6).

And therefore the grave could not hold Him.

The Resurrection and New Life

He had to rise again!

And on the third day, He did! (Acts 2:24; 1 Corinthians 15:4).

And when He did, He conquered death. Which means that whoever believes in Him does not have to face the final death, which is separation from God forever (John 11:25–26; Revelation 20:14–15).

Not only that, but those who trust in Christ are forgiven all their sins and counted righteous before God, not because they have been good enough, but because of what Christ has done for them (Romans 5:1; 2 Corinthians 5:21).

Basically, Christ has overcome the curse brought into the world by sin, and now those who trust in Him to save them are reunited to God. That lost communion is restored!

And this is not simply a promise about what happens when we die. Those who believe in Christ are brought into fellowship with God now, given new life by the Holy Spirit, and adopted into God's family (John 1:12–13; Romans 8:15–16).

All believers will therefore live with God forever.

And one day, Christ will return, raise His people from the dead, and make all things new. The curse brought about by sin will be gone forever, and God's people will live with Him in a new creation where there is no more sin, suffering, or death (Revelation 21:1–4).

Will You Believe?

The really important question is then, will you believe in Jesus Christ as your Saviour?

God commands us to repent and believe the Gospel (Mark 1:15). That means turning away from our sin and turning to Jesus Christ, trusting Him alone to save us.

Will you say, "Yes, I'm a sinner, but Jesus Christ died to save sinners, therefore I will trust in Him"?

You cannot make yourself good enough for God. You cannot undo the wrong things you have done. But Jesus Christ came into the world to save sinners (1 Timothy 1:15).

And He promises that whoever comes to Him, He will never cast out (John 6:37).
